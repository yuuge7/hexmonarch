# HexMonarch field manual

How to play, what to do first, and what everything on screen means.

## The game in one minute

You build a criminal empire on the real map. You **plant turfs** on the spot where you are standing, or on open ground near a turf you already hold. Each one earns money every hour, even while the phone is off. Some turfs become **Anchor Hubs** that protect the turfs around them. Hubs in different towns are linked with **relays**, and the more towns you link, the more everything earns. Rival crews hold red turfs, raid yours, and push back harder as you grow. There is no end: every number keeps scaling.

## Your first 15 minutes

Do these in order.

- **1. Plant your first turf.** Open the game where you are right now. The big green button says "Plant turf here". Press it. Cost: 60 credits.
- **2. Make it an Anchor Hub.** With that turf on screen, press "Make this an anchor hub" (300 credits, 120 materials). A hub supplies every turf within 2.5 km, which is most of a town. Supplied turfs earn full income and repair themselves.
- **3. Spend your energy.** Go to **Crew > Jobs** and run jobs until energy is empty. Jobs are your main income for the first days. You are standing in your own turf, so they pay x1.5.
- **4. Plant around you.** You do not have to stand on the exact spot. Tap any open ground inside the dotted green ring, or inside the thin green outline around your turfs, then press "Plant turf there". Turfs must sit 100 m apart, so the dimmed patches around existing turfs are off limits. Stay inside the dashed amber circle (your hub's supply sphere) so the new turfs are protected.
- **5. Buy your first perk.** In **Crew > Perks**, buy **Deep Bench** for more job energy, or **Long Arm** to plant even farther out.
- **6. Turn on auto-plant.** In **Network > Field ops**, set "Auto-plant while travelling" to **Stations**, and switch on **Patrol mode**. Now every station you pass on a train or bus gets a turf, even with the phone in your pocket.
- **7. Allow alerts.** The game asks once to send notifications. Say yes: it is how you hear that a turf was raided while the game is closed.

After that the daily loop is: spend energy on jobs, collect what the Event Deck offers nearby, plant where you travel, upgrade your hub.

## Reading the map

- **White dot**: you.
- **Dashed green ring around you**: a new turf would land here. No ring means you are too close to another turf.
- **Wide dotted green ring around you**: your plant range. Tap open ground inside it to plant there.
- **Thin green outline around your turfs**: the reach of your turfs. Open ground inside it can be planted from anywhere, without walking there.
- **Dimmed patches inside that ring**: too close to an existing turf, nothing can be planted there.
- **Green circle**: your turf, supplied by a hub.
- **Dashed green circle**: your turf with no hub in reach.
- **Amber circle**: your Anchor Hub. The big dashed amber circle around it is its supply sphere.
- **Inner ring inside a turf**: it is a station turf.
- **Small line under a turf's name** (zoom in): what that turf mainly supplies per hour. Amber CR is credits, white MAT is materials, blue INT is intel.
- **Red circle**: rival turf.
- **Amber dashed line with moving dots**: a relay between two hubs.
- **Spinning amber diamond**: a convoy. **Blue diamond**: a dead drop. The dotted ring around them is how close you must get.
- **Red target on a hub**: an offensive is coming.
- **Red dashed outline over a large area**: that district is locked down.

Tap a turf to inspect it. Tap the X in the console header to go back to your own position.

Four buttons float on the map, bottom right:

- **Layers**: what the map shows. See Map layers below. The button lights up green when you are not on the everyday Tactical view.
- **Sun**: switches to a white map with dark markings. Use it outside when the sun washes out the dark map. Tap again to go back.
- **Frame**: shows your whole empire.
- **Target**: re-centers on you and follows you.

## Map layers

Tap **Layers** on the map. The map changes behind the sheet as you switch things, and the game remembers your choice.

Three ready-made modes:

- **Clean**: turfs, names, relays and events. Nothing else on the streets.
- **Tactical**: the everyday view, and the one the game starts with. Adds supply spheres and the reach outline.
- **Intel**: everything below.

Or switch single layers:

- **Names**: turf names, and close in, what each one supplies per hour.
- **Supply spheres**: the big dashed amber circle each hub supplies.
- **Reach outline**: open ground you can plant from your turfs.
- **Relays**: the dashed amber lines between hubs.
- **Supply lines**: a thin green line from every turf to the hub that supplies it. A turf without one has no hub.
- **Reach web**: dotted green lines between your turfs that are within plant range of each other. It shows how your empire chains outward, turf by turf.
- **Integrity rings**: an arc around each turf, full at 100%. Green above 60%, amber above 30%, red below. A red dashed ring means the turf is under siege. Rival turfs get one too once a failed breach has worn them down.
- **Breach odds**: your chance, in percent, under every rival turf (zoom in to see them). Green is 60% or more, amber 35% or more, red below. Grey means nothing of yours reaches it yet.
- **Ground type**: the centre of each turf is filled with the colour of what its ground pays: amber credits, white materials, blue intel.
- **Field events**: convoys, dead drops, offensives and locked-down districts.

Turf circles and your own position are always shown.

## Map only, tall console, and the turf list

- **Tall console**: the double arrow in the console header makes the console take most of the screen, with a strip of map on top. Use it to read and manage; tap it again for the normal split. The map buttons move into a row while it is tall. The game remembers the choice.
- **Map only**: the arrow at the right of the console header folds the console away, so the main screen is just the map. Tapping the tab that is already open does the same. Tap any tab, the arrow or the header strip to bring the console back. While it is folded, tapping a turf only shows its name in the strip. The game remembers the choice.
- **Turf list**: the list icon in the console header (also **Network > All my turfs**) shows every turf you hold with the credits, materials and intel it supplies per hour, its integrity and its distance. Sort by nearest, name, credits, materials, intel or weakest. Filter to hubs, stations, turfs with no hub, or turfs rivals took from you. Tap a row to jump to that turf on the map.

## The six tabs

- **Turf**: the spot you stand on, or the turf you tapped. Plant, upgrade, breach, rename. See The Turf tab below.
- **Empire**: all your turfs at once. Upgrade garrisons or hubs on every turf in one go, repair everything, fill every socket. See The Empire tab below.
- **Network**: income per hour, your hubs and relays, rival pressure, settings, this manual, save export.
- **Crew**: Jobs, Perks, Modules, Shop, Trade.
- **Ops**: the Event Deck (things to go and do) and the feed of what happened.
- **Vault**: the long game. Network Liquidation and permanent upgrades.

The strip at the top shows credits (CR), materials (MAT), intel (INT), energy (NRG), cryptokeys, and your level.

## The Turf tab

Tap one of your turfs. The top shows what it is (hub or turf, its class or garrison level), how far away it is, and its tags. Under that, three sections:

- **Overview**: whether a hub supplies it, any siege, what it supplies per hour and why, its defense and integrity, and for a hub its sphere, relay range and relays.
- **Modules**: its sockets (tap an empty one to pick a module, the amber tile to buy one more), and **Fill from stash**, which puts your best stash modules into its empty sockets.
- **Manage**: the next 1, 5, 10 and 25 garrison levels, each with its full price and the output it adds; the same for hub levels on a hub; the relay screen; making it a hub; abandon and delete.

The bar at the bottom always holds its main move: the next hub level on a hub, the next garrison level on a turf (or making your first hub), plus Repair when it is damaged. The end of the list fades out above the bar, so nothing is cut in half.

## The Empire tab

For when you hold dozens of turfs and do not want to open each one.

- **Your empire**: turfs, hubs, average garrison (with the lowest and highest), how many are supplied, damaged or under siege, empty sockets, and what it all pays per hour.
- **Upgrade many at once**: pick **Garrisons** or **Hub levels**. For garrisons, pick which turfs: all, plain turfs, hubs, stations, those with no hub, or damaged ones (the number on each chip is how many). Then **Add levels** (+1 to +100 on each) or **Up to level** (every one below that level is raised to it; the others are left alone). The screen shows the levels, the full price and what it adds per hour.
- **Hold: upgrade all** buys the lot when you can pay for it. Hold the button.
- **Max affordable** spends what you have instead: the lowest levels first across all of them, until the next level is more than you hold. Example: 100 turfs at garrison 1 and enough money for 150 levels gives every turf level 2 and half of them level 3. The screen says how many of the levels your funds cover.
- Upgrading in bulk costs exactly what upgrading one by one costs, and gives the same XP.
- **Upkeep**: **Repair** brings every damaged turf to 100% and lifts their sieges (the same materials as repairing them one by one). **Fill** puts the best modules from your stash into every empty socket, hubs first, then the turfs that pay most.

## Turfs

- You can plant anywhere you physically are. No need to connect to your other turfs.
- New turfs must be 100 m from any other turf, yours or a rival's. The **Close Quarters** perk shrinks that.
- Your plant range is 250 m, and the 100 m no-plant gap does not count against it: you can plant up to 350 m from where you stand, so even standing on a turf you have a full 250 m of usable ground. The **Long Arm** perk adds more.
- Every turf you hold has the same range. See **Reaching out from your turfs** below.
- The price rises a little with every turf you own.
- **Garrison** upgrades raise a turf's income, defense and size.
- A turf is named after the nearest station, landmark or street. Tap the pencil to rename it.
- Getting rid of a turf: open it and scroll down. **Hold to abandon** removes it, but a rival crew that used to live there moves back in, and auto-plant may plant it again when you pass. **Hold to delete** removes it for good: no crew returns and auto-plant skips the spot until you plant there by hand. Both send the modules to your stash; neither gives a refund.
- Each neighbourhood has a type: **Commercial Transit** (credits), **Municipal Records** (intel), **Industrial Yards** (materials). About one block in seven hides an **anomaly** such as a Bunker (+50% defense) or a Black Market (+50% credits, more raids).

## What a turf supplies

Tap one of your turfs. The amber box at the top of the Turf tab says what it supplies you every hour and every day: credits, materials and intel, each on its own.

- Under the three numbers is the reason: what the ground gives on its own, then every multiplier in play. For example Garrison x1.35, Hub x2.60, Trade x1.30, No hub x0.40, Station x1.30 cr, Surge x2.00, Lockdown.
- **Commercial Transit** ground gives 10 credits, 0.6 materials and 0.2 intel per hour before multipliers. **Municipal Records** gives 3, 0.8 and 2. **Industrial Yards** gives 3, 4 and 0.2.
- **Network > Who supplies what** adds it up per type of ground, so you can see which kind of turf you are short of.
- The turf list shows all three numbers for every turf and can sort by any of them.

## Reaching out from your turfs

Like in Turf Wars, your turfs work for you where they stand. You do not have to be there.

- **Plant**: tap open ground within 350 m of any turf you hold (the thin green outline) and press "Plant turf there". It works from home, and with no GPS fix at all. The new turf reaches another 350 m, so an empire can grow outward turf by turf.
- **Attack**: tap a rival turf within 350 m of one of yours and press Breach. The chip "In reach of ..." names the turf the strike comes from.
- **Collect**: a convoy or a dead drop within 350 m of one of your turfs can be taken from the Ops tab without walking to it.
- The **Long Arm** perk stretches all three, for you and for every turf. **Deep Reach** adds to attacks and pickups.
- Lockdowns can be lifted from anywhere too: it costs x1.5 unless you stand in the district.
- Walking is still worth it: far towns can only be started in person, jobs pay x1.5 inside your own turf, and auto-plant claims stations as you pass them.

## Stations are special

A turf planted within 250 m of a train, metro or tram station (150 m for bus stations and ferry terminals) becomes a **station turf**:

- +30% credits.
- It needs no hub: it keeps 70% output and never decays on its own.
- As a hub, its relay range is x1.5.
- Only one turf per station gets the bonus. Further turfs near it are ordinary ones, named after their street.

This is why a turf at every station on your line is the backbone of the game.

## Hubs and supply

- Any turf can become a hub. The first costs 300 credits and 120 materials, each later one costs 1.4x more (the tenth is about 6,200 credits).
- A hub supplies every turf of yours inside its sphere: full income, self-repair, and a share of the hub's defense.
- The sphere is 2.5 km at level 1, 3 km at level 4, 3.5 km at level 9. One hub near the middle of a town like Târgoviște holds a turf in every neighbourhood, so spread your turfs out instead of packing them around the hub.
- A normal turf with no hub earns 40% and wears down to 30% integrity, which makes it easy prey for raids. It does not vanish by itself.
- Upgrade a hub to widen its sphere, lengthen its relay range, add module sockets and raise defense.
- Good hub spots: a station in the middle of an area you walk a lot. A big city like Bucharest needs several hubs.

## Districts, relays and trade

- The map is divided into districts roughly the size of a town (about 17 km across). Târgoviște, Pitești, Titu, Găești are each their own district; Bucharest covers two or three.
- A relay links one hub to another. Each hub can hold up to **3 outgoing relays**, each to a different hub; two hubs need only one relay between them. Open the hub, press **Relay**: the pips at the top show the slots in use, then the network's hubs, districts, relays and trade multiplier. Every linked hub in the list has a **Sever** button to free its slot. Range is 10 km + 2.5 km per hub level, x1.5 from a station hub: a level 1 hub reaches 12.5 km (shown as 13 km), a level 1 station hub 19 km, a level 5 station hub 34 km.
- **Stuck at 13 km?** Two ways to reach farther. Upgrade the hub the relay starts from. Or buy the **Signal Boost** perk (Crew > Perks): +20% relay range per rank on every hub, no limit. Rank 3 turns 12.5 km into 20 km; rank 5 doubles it. The relay screen shows the hub's current range at the top.
- All linked hubs form a network. Every extra district in a network adds +30% income to every turf that network supplies; every relay adds +4%.
- So one hub in Bucharest linked to one in Titu and one in Târgoviște earns far more than three hubs in the same town. Long distances need higher hub levels or a chain of hubs along the railway (Gara de Nord, Chitila, Titu, Găești, Pitești).

## Jobs and energy

- Energy refills by 15 per hour up to 30. Full energy is wasted energy.
- Each job costs energy and pays credits, XP and sometimes intel or materials. Bigger jobs unlock as you level.
- Stand inside one of your own turfs for x1.5 pay.
- Every 10 runs of the same job raises its mastery: +5% pay, forever.
- Every run has a small chance to drop a module.
- Jobs matter most early. Later your turfs out-earn them, but jobs stay the fastest XP.

## Perks

Bought in **Crew > Perks** with credits and intel. Perks are permanent, they even survive a Network Liquidation.

- **Long Arm**: plant farther out, from where you stand and from every turf you hold. Everyone starts with 250 m of reach; rank 1 makes it 330 m, rank 2 is 390 m, rank 5 is 540 m, and it keeps growing. The dotted ring around you and the outline around your turfs show the range.
- **Deep Reach**: breach rivals and grab convoys and drops from farther away, on foot and from your turfs.
- **Close Quarters**: turfs can sit closer together (from 100 m down to 60 m).
- **Signal Boost**: +20% relay range per rank on every hub. This is how you link towns that are out of range.
- **Muscle**: +8% strike, +4% defense per rank.
- **Recon**: rival turfs defend 5% weaker per rank, up to 10 ranks (-50%). Rank 1 costs 250 credits and 20 intel.
- **Deep Bench**: +10 maximum energy per rank.
- **Second Wind**: +10% energy regeneration per rank.
- **Street Smarts**: +5% XP per rank.
- **Safehouses**: hub-less turfs hold more integrity (30% up to 90%).

What to buy first: Deep Bench and Second Wind (more jobs per day), then Long Arm, then Signal Boost once your second town is out of relay range, then Muscle and Recon when rivals start winning.

## Rivals

- Red turfs belong to six rival factions. Walk into one, or have a turf of your own within 350 m of it, and press **Breach**. The button shows your odds. Win and the turf is yours; you often loot a module.
- The factions are not equally strong. The three old ones fight at full strength. The **Corner Boys** (55% strength) are the most common and the easiest to take. The **Scrappers** fight at 75%. **The Old Firm** fights at 130% and is rare, but every turf you take from it drops a Rare or better module. Opening a rival turf tells you which kind it is. Their names change with the tier. The three newer factions raid you half as often as the old ones.
- About one block in eight starts with a rival turf on it (one in eleven before 0.6).
- Rivals **raid** your turfs every hour. A raid that beats the turf's defense damages it by at least 30%. Turfs without a hub are hit more often: station turfs twice as often, ordinary ones four times.
- If they take a turf, its hub level, modules and bought sockets stay on it. Breach it to get everything back.
- **Network > Rival crews** lists all six factions: how hard each one fights, how often they have won and lost against you, and any nemesis rank. Every 25 raids a faction wins add a nemesis rank: +10% strength.
- **Rival pressure** (Network tab) fills up while things are quiet. When it is full, a faction announces an **offensive** on one of your hubs. You have 10 hours to press **Fortify** in the Ops tab.
- When rivals **raid** you, their strength follows the level of your best hub. They never fall behind.
- When you **breach** them, a rival turf only fights as hard as the hub that covers it: the strongest of your hubs whose supply sphere reaches it. Rival turfs far from your hubs fight as if you had none, so a level 20 hub at home does not make the crews in another town any harder. Rival turfs inside a big hub's sphere are tougher, but that hub backs your strike too.

## Breaching: how to hit harder

Your odds are your strike against their defense, between 5% and 95%. Open a rival turf to see both as one bar (green is your share), then line by line where your strike comes from and what has weakened them. The chip at the top says how hard that faction fights.

- **Level**: 20, plus 12 per level.
- **Hub**: half the defense of your strongest hub whose sphere covers the target.
- **Backup**: half the defense of the best-garrisoned turf you hold within 350 m of the target. A garrison 10 turf adds about 120, garrison 25 about 950. Upgrade the garrison of the turf you attack from.
- **Muscle** (Crew > Perks): +8% strike per rank.
- **Hired Guns** (Crew > Shop): x1.5 strike for 3 hours.
- On their side, **Recon** (Crew > Perks) takes 5% per rank off every rival turf's defense, and **Inside Man** (Crew > Shop) another 30% for 3 hours.
- **Wear them down**: a failed breach still knocks 25% off the rival turf's integrity, and a turf at 0% defends at half strength. Failed breaches no longer count toward a faction's nemesis rank.

## Sieges: you cannot lose a turf behind your back

Rivals can never hit a turf for the first time and take it while you are away, however long you are away.

- The first raid that damages a turf puts it **under siege**. From then on, a raid that would bring it to 0% leaves it standing at **5%** instead.
- It can only be taken after you have opened the game since the siege began. Twelve hours away, raided in hour two, raided again in hour nine: you come back to a turf at 5%, not to a red one.
- Once you have opened the game and seen it, the next raid that breaks the turf takes it. So act: press **Repair** on the turf (materials), or let its hub mend it. At 100% the siege is lifted and the next raid starts from scratch.
- The same goes for offensives. One that was announced while you were away can wreck the hub and the turfs within 400 m of it, down to 5%, but cannot take them. One you saw announced and ignored can.
- Besieged turfs are marked in the Turf tab and the turf list, and the "While you were dark" report counts them.

## Alerts

The game sends a notification when something needs you, even when it is closed. No internet is used: the game works out ahead of time what the coming three days hold and sets the alarms itself.

- **Attacks** (with sound): a turf was hit, a turf is holding at 5%, a turf or a hub was lost, an offensive was announced or has landed.
- **Opportunities** (silent): a convoy or a dead drop appeared, a district was locked down, your energy is full.
- Switch them in **Network > Field ops > Alerts**. If the switch says alerts are blocked, flip it and allow notifications for HexMonarch in the system screen that opens.
- Android lets you mute Attacks or Opportunities separately: long-press a notification.
- Alerts can arrive a few minutes late when the phone is in deep sleep. With the game open you get a banner on screen instead.

## The Event Deck

Cards are dealt four times a day, at **08:00, 12:00, 16:00 and 20:00** by the phone's clock, and never at night, so nothing appears and runs out while you sleep. The morning deal brings 2 or 3 cards of any kind; the later ones 1 or 2 convoys, dead drops or surges. That is 5 to 9 cards a day. Find them in **Ops**.

The strip at the top of Ops shows today: green diamonds are deals already made, the amber one is the next, the white marker is now, and the shaded ends are the night. Each card has a bar showing how much of its time is left.

- **Lockdown**: one district's income is frozen. It lifts by itself after **24 hours**, or pay it off in Ops: from anywhere for x1.5, or at the normal price while you stand in the district. Morning only, only once you hold 5 turfs, and never more than one district in four at a time.
- **Convoy**: appears near one of your hubs for **6 hours**. Get within 150 m, or have a turf within 350 m of it, and intercept it for blueprints, credits and a rare module. Up to 3 at a time.
- **Dead drop**: within a kilometre of where you last were, for 24 hours. Walk to within 100 m, or have a turf within 350 m of it. Up to 4 at a time.
- **Surge**: one turf type produces double for 12 hours.
- **Market**: credit income x1.5 or x0.6 for 24 hours. Morning only.
- **Offensive**: see Rivals. Morning only, from level 5.

## Modules

- Loot with random stats. Socket them into a turf: tap your turf, then an empty socket.
- A module on a hub also boosts every turf the hub supplies.
- A turf starts with 1 socket (+1 per 10 garrison levels), a hub with 2 (+1 per 5 hub levels). **Buy a socket** adds one more to any turf: tap the amber tile next to its sockets, or use **Crew > Trade > Socket expander**. The first costs 1,200 credits, 400 materials and 100 intel; each further one on the same turf costs double. There is no limit.
- Scrap what you do not need for materials.
- **Blueprints** (from convoys) can be decrypted in Crew > Modules into a guaranteed Rare or better module.
- Rarities: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic. There is no best module, a stronger roll always exists.

## Trade

**Crew > Trade** is the exchange. Swap credits, materials and intel for one another.

- Values: 1 material is worth 2.5 credits, 1 intel is worth 6 credits. The fence keeps 25% of every trade. So 100 credits buy 30 materials or 12.5 intel, and 100 materials sell for 187 credits.
- Pick what you give, what you get and how much (10%, 25%, 50% or Max of what you hold).
- There is a daily limit, counted in credits' worth: 1,000, plus 250 per level above 1, plus 12 hours of what your turfs produce. It resets every day at midnight UTC (03:00 in Romanian summer time, 02:00 in winter). To raise it, level up and grow your hourly output.
- Trading there and back loses the fee twice. Use it to fix a shortage, not to make money.
- The **Socket expander** is on the same screen: see Modules.

## Shop

**Crew > Shop** sells boosts and supplies for credits, materials and intel. Prices follow your hourly output (credits, plus materials at 2.5 and intel at 6 each, as on the exchange), so they mean something at every stage. Each repeat buy of the same item on the same day costs 50% more; Energy Drink doubles instead. Prices reset with the exchange limit.

- **Hired Guns**: x1.5 strike on every breach for 3 hours. One hour of output in credits (at least 200), plus 10 intel and 2 more per level.
- **Inside Man**: rival turfs defend 30% weaker for 3 hours. Three quarters of an hour of output in credits (at least 150), plus 20 intel and 4 more per level.
- **Barricades**: x1.5 defense on every turf you hold for 12 hours. Two hours of output in credits (at least 300) and a quarter of an hour in materials (at least 60).
- **Ceasefire**: no raids on any of your turfs for 6 hours. Offensives already announced still land. Three hours of output in credits (at least 500), plus 15 intel and 3 more per level. Raids start again on the hour after it ends.
- **Repair Crew**: every damaged turf back to 100%, wherever it is, and their sieges lifted. 75% of the materials repairing them one by one would cost.
- **Energy Drink**: energy refilled to the maximum. Half an hour of output in credits (at least 100).
- **Supply Crate**: one module, Uncommon or better. Three hours of output in credits (at least 400) and half an hour in materials (at least 80).
- **Blueprint**: one blueprint to decrypt in Crew > Modules. Four hours of output in credits (at least 600), plus 30 intel and 5 more per level.

Buying a boost that is already running adds its time on top. Boosts that are running show as green tags under the resource strip at the top of the screen, with the time left, and as a time bar on their card in the shop.

## Where to spend

- **Credits**: first hub, then turfs, then hub levels. Keep enough back for the next plant when you travel.
- **Materials**: hubs and garrisons eat them. Industrial turfs and the "Boost a delivery van" job supply them.
- **Intel**: perks and breaches. Municipal turfs supply it.
- Short on one and swimming in another? **Crew > Trade**.
- Rivals too strong, or a town full of damage? **Crew > Shop**.

## The long game

- **Tiers** change the story, not the screen. Level 20: the Corporate Syndicate. Level 50: the Sci-Fi Escalation. After that a new Epoch every 50 levels.
- **Network Liquidation** (Vault tab) sells the whole empire for **Offshore Cryptokeys**. It needs 50 turfs, a network linking 3 regions (areas about 45 km across, think Bucharest, Târgoviște, Pitești), and a level 30 hub. You lose turfs, modules and resources; you keep cryptokeys, vault upgrades and perks. Cryptokeys buy permanent bonuses with no rank limit.

## Time, offline play and the clock

- The empire keeps running while the app is closed, at 60% income. Raids and events happen too. You get a report when you return.
- The game uses the phone's internal timer, not the date. Changing the date or time earns nothing and gets flagged.

## Changing phones

Your empire lives only on your phone. To move it:

- On the old phone: **Network > Move to another phone > Export save**. Pick where to put the file (Google Drive is the easiest).
- On the new phone: install the game, open it once, then **Network > Move to another phone**, hold the **Import** button and pick that file.

Export a save now and then as a backup too. Importing replaces whatever is on the phone.

## Common questions

- **The plant button is grey.** The spot is within 100 m of another turf (pick a spot outside the dimmed patches), more than 350 m from you and from every turf you hold, or you are short on credits or materials (the missing one is shown in red). Long Arm stretches the 350 m.
- **A relay says "Out of range".** Upgrade the hub it starts from, or buy Signal Boost in Crew > Perks. See Districts, relays and trade.
- **I cannot read the screen in the sun.** Tap the sun button on the map for the white map.
- **The map is too busy, or I want to see more.** Tap the Layers button on the map: Clean for less, Intel for everything, or switch single layers.
- **The bottom half of the screen is always in the way.** Tap the arrow in the console header for map only.
- **My income says +0/h.** A lockdown has frozen the district. Check Ops: pay it off from wherever you are, or wait the 24 hours.
- **Every breach is 15%.** Open the rival turf and read where your strike comes from. Garrison up the turf of yours next to it, take Muscle and Recon, use Hired Guns and Inside Man, or pick on the Corner Boys first. A failed breach still wears them down.
- **A turf turned red.** A rival took it. Breach it: walk in, or tap it if one of your turfs is within 350 m.
- **A turf is stuck at 5%.** It is under siege. Repair it before the next raid. See Sieges.
- **I get no notifications.** Network > Field ops > Alerts, and allow notifications for HexMonarch in Android's settings.
- **Do I have to keep the app open?** No. For auto-plant on a train turn on Patrol mode; it keeps GPS running with a notification.
- **Does it need internet?** Only to download map tiles the first time you see an area. Use "Cache 5 km around me" at home before going offline.
